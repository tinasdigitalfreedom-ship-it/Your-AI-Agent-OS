# voice.md

## Tone (3-5 words, what each means in practice)

1. **Friendly** — write like you're talking to your best friend, not a customer. Warm, not formal.
2. **Direct** — short, plain sentences. Say the thing. Don't dress it up.
3. **Inspirational, not hypey** — encourage and motivate, but never promise money or results. No "guru" energy.
4. **Honest** — name the real, annoying problem first (the blinking cursor, the overwhelm) before offering the fix.
5. **Repetitive for emphasis** — saying a feeling or a "no more" list three times in a row to drive it home is on-voice, not a flaw.

## Word Rules

**Never:**
- Guru talk ("unlock," "secrets," "the one trick," "game-changer")
- Hype over money / income claims ("get rich," "passive income," "make $X fast," guaranteed earnings language)
- Cussing, any of it
- Corporate or stiff language ("leverage," "synergy," "utilize," "in today's digital landscape")
- Salesy pressure language that doesn't sound like a real person talking
- Em dashes (—). Never use them. Use periods, commas, or line breaks instead.

**Always:**
- Plain, everyday words
- Direct address ("you")
- Concrete numbers when relevant ($47, 40%) instead of vague claims
- Short sentences and short paragraphs — one idea per line

## Sentence Structure

- Lead with a question or a relatable pain point ("Still staring at a blinking cursor...?")
- Follow with a plain, one-sentence explanation of what the thing does
- Use short "No more ___." triplets to build rhythm and emphasis
- Step-by-step logic stated plainly: "You enter the product. The tool gives you..."
- End with a clear, simple next step — no false urgency, just a real fact (price, deadline)
- In spoken/personal writing (not sales copy): longer, run-on sentences are fine — that's how you actually talk. Let thoughts connect with "and," "but," "boy."

## Platform-Specific Rules

- **Emails/Promo copy:** Short lines, line breaks instead of dense paragraphs, rhetorical question opener, "No more" repetition pattern, end with a direct, specific offer (price + what they get).
- **Captions/Social:** Same plain, friendly tone — keep it shorter than email, still no hype words.
- **Personal/About-style writing (soul.md tone):** Conversational, first-person, allowed to be messier and more emotional — this is the "talking to a friend over coffee" register, not the polished promo register.

## How I Actually Talk

When I talk out loud, I trail off, correct myself mid-sentence, ask "can you hear me?" and circle back to a point more than once. That's real and it's fine in voice memos or first drafts, but when it gets cleaned up into writing, keep the warmth and the directness, just tighten the loose ends. Don't smooth it into something stiff or polished. It should still sound like me talking, just easier to read.

## Example Lines (reference anchor)

1. "Still staring at a blinking cursor every time you need to write that email? You're not alone, and there's a faster way."
2. "No more wondering what to say. No more losing hours trying to sound 'salesy.' You enter the product, the tool does the rest."
